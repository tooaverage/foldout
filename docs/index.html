Foldout

The whole page, at your screen's own resolution

Foldout photographs an entire web page, not just the part that fits on screen. Click once and it scrolls the page for you, captures every screenful, and stitches them into a single image you can save or copy.

What makes it different

  • It never shrinks anything. Captures happen at your display's own pixel density, so a Retina screen gives you a Retina image. Pages too tall for one image are split into several full-resolution pieces rather than blurred to fit.
  • Sticky headers are handled. Your navigation bar appears once at the top, instead of being stamped across every screenful.
  • It says what it is doing. Live progress, an explanation on the page itself, and Escape to stop.

Why it cannot spy on you

Foldout requests exactly two permissions. activeTab grants access to a single tab, only at the moment you click the icon. scripting allows it to call Chrome's injection API, and grants access to nothing on its own.

It declares no host_permissions, which is the setting that would let an extension read sites in the background. And it contains no fetch, no XMLHttpRequest, no WebSocket and no remotely loaded script, so there is no code path capable of sending an image anywhere.

You do not have to take that on trust. The source is public and short enough to read in a sitting.

Getting it

Foldout is being prepared for the Chrome Web Store. Until then you can load it yourself: download the source, open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, and choose Load unpacked.
